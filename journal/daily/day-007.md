## 4. IoU Diagnostic Findings

The dataset audit showed that bounding-box overlap strongly affected the measured results.

At IoU 0.30, the diagnostic results were:

### Cars

- True positives: 60
- False positives: 22
- False negatives: 13
- Precision: 0.732
- Recall: 0.822
- F1 score: 0.774

### Trucks

- True positives: 9
- False positives: 5
- False negatives: 4
- Precision: 0.643
- Recall: 0.692
- F1 score: 0.667

The audit also found that 33 car annotations and one truck annotation had a best independent IoU between 0.30 and 0.50.

This suggests that imperfect bounding-box overlap is an important contributor to the lower scores at IoU 0.50.

The IoU 0.30 results are diagnostic only. The official baseline remains the evaluation at IoU 0.50.

## 5. Interpretation and Limitations

The experiment established a measurable baseline for the current pretrained detector.

Car detection agreement remains limited. Truck results are better in this sample, but the dataset contains only 13 labelled trucks, so the results are not sufficient to establish reliable truck-detection performance.

The audit identified annotations whose best independent overlap with a prediction was below 0.30. These cases require further inspection.

An unmatched prediction is not automatically a genuine false detection: it could also reflect an omitted manual label, a poorly aligned bounding box, or a classification issue. Similarly, a missed manual annotation does not necessarily mean that the vehicle was completely invisible to the model.

The evaluation dataset is small and consists of frames sampled from a single test video. The results should not be generalized to all traffic scenes.

## 6. Key Lessons Learned

- A pretrained model can be evaluated without training it.
- Precision measures the proportion of predictions that match the reference annotations.
- Recall measures the proportion of reference objects successfully matched.
- F1 score combines precision and recall.
- IoU determines how much a predicted box must overlap a manual box to count as a match.
- Annotation consistency is essential for trustworthy evaluation.
- A change in evaluation metrics does not, by itself, prove that the model has improved.

## 8. Day 7 Summary

Day 7 established the first quantitative vehicle-detection baseline for RoadFlow AI using 10 manually labelled traffic frames.

The official evaluation produced an F1 score of 0.348 for cars and 0.593 for trucks at a confidence threshold of 0.25 and an IoU threshold of 0.50.

The audit demonstrated that bounding-box overlap has a substantial effect on measured performance. The results also identified dataset-size and annotation-quality limitations that must be addressed before drawing strong conclusions or training a specialized detector.

**The goal is not simply to make an AI model run. The goal is to understand its behavior, measure its limitations, and build a reliable traffic-intelligence system one step at a time.**
